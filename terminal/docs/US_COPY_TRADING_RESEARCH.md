# Polymarket US copy trading feasibility

Research checked October 17, 2026. Scope: US Bitcoin Up/Down 15-minute and one-hour markets; identifying successful source traders and observing their trades early enough to copy them. Only first-party documentation was used for external factual claims. No credentials, `.env` files, authenticated account data, runtime modules, or orders were accessed or changed. This note does not contain a trader ranking.

## Finding

**The reviewed US interfaces do not establish a public, attributable trader feed or leaderboard from which to build the requested high-win-rate US copy-trading cohort.** Public market data and anonymous trade prints are available; account histories are permissioned. International wallet leaderboards are a different data population and cannot be represented as US traders. This is a documented-interface limitation, not proof that no private commercial arrangement or future feature could support the use case.

The strongest direct evidence is the US [Reporting Data guide](https://docs.polymarket.us/trader-guide/reporting): trade search returns only the authenticated participant's authorized accounts, not every exchange trade. Separately, the [US changelog](https://docs.polymarket.us/changelog), May 21, 2026, v0.0.28, announces removal of usernames from Retail trade-tape responses. These facts prevent the normal public-wallet discovery → historical scoring → live attribution workflow.

### RFQ identifiers are a narrow exception, not a solution

US [Get RFQ user ID](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/rfqs/get-rfq-user-id) returns a public RFQ identifier for the authenticated participant. Therefore, claiming that US has no public participant identifiers of any kind would be too broad. However, [Get RFQ trades](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/rfqs/get-rfq-trades) explicitly returns anonymous fills. Its response has trade ID, symbol, price, quantity, aggressor side and execution time, with no source participant identifier. The docs also warn that history appears eventually and later corrections/busts do not amend those prints. RFQ visibility is not evidence of a public PnL history, a comprehensive trader roster, or attributable ordinary BTC Up/Down executions.

## US BTC 15m/1h identification and availability

The official [Crypto Schema](https://docs.polymarket.us/trader-guide/crypto-schema) identifies automated US contracts through typed metadata: BTC asset, BRTI index, `crypto_market_type=updown`, `crypto_horizon=15m` or `1h`, and `interval_start`/`interval_end`. Series are `btc-updown-15m` and `btc-updown-1h`. In Retail, use typed `assetPriceTerms`, including market type and window boundaries; do not classify by loose title text or confuse hourly strike ladders with one-hour Up/Down windows.

The [Crypto FAQs](https://docs.polymarket.us/faqs/crypto-faqs) describe both cadences, BRTI-based settlement and Up winning ties, but retain a preprod/production-rollout caveat. The September 21 [changelog](https://docs.polymarket.us/changelog) likewise labels the families preprod/upcoming. Documentation alone therefore does not prove a currently tradeable production inventory. The existing [BRTI follow-up note](BRTI_FOLLOWUP_RESEARCH.md) separately records bounded production GET observations of resolved 15m/1h markets on October 16; it also records unresolved reference reconstruction discrepancies. This research did not rerun those observations. Live availability, permissions, book liquidity and exact rules must be rechecked against the intended production contract before any future execution.

## International data must remain separate

Polymarket's [US welcome page](https://docs.polymarket.us/getting-started/welcome) explicitly links to separate International documentation. The following public-wallet capabilities are documented in that International documentation:

| International interface | What it exposes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| [`data-api.polymarket.com/v1/leaderboard`](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/core/get-trader-leaderboard-rankings) | Wallet/profile address, username, volume, PnL and rank, with category, time period and PnL/volume ordering. The page points to a v2 counterpart. | It is not a US leaderboard. The documented v1 row has no win-rate or sample-size field. A broad `CRYPTO` filter is not BTC 15m/1h specialization. |
| [`data-api.polymarket.com/trades`](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/core/get-trades-for-a-user-or-markets) | Wallet-attributed history, market/asset/outcome, side, size, price, timestamp and transaction hash; user/market filters. The documented default is `takerOnly=true`. | It is not a permissionless US account-history API. A default taker-only sample is not a complete trading record. |
| [Data API v2 specification](https://data-api.polymarket.com/v2/docs) | Public no-auth wallet data, feeds and boards with cursor pagination, explicit window rules and service freshness metadata. | Newer versioning does not turn these wallet datasets into US records or guarantee profitable copies. |

**Inference:** Using International traders as cross-venue signals would be a separate strategy, not direct US trader copying. It would require explicit contract mapping, settlement-rule comparison and observed price/latency tests. The reviewed sources provide no established identity mapping from an International wallet to a US participant, and no evidence that their trade histories or rankings transfer.

## Required dataset before claiming a high-win-rate cohort

The following are proposed research requirements, not reported exchange capabilities or verified trader results:

1. **Attribution and coverage:** Stable, authorized source identity; venue and environment; complete entries, reductions, exits, partial fills, settlement, corrections/busts and fees. Preserve source IDs and raw payload hashes. Record missing pages, outages, late arrivals and the first/last covered dates. Unknown values stay unknown, not zero. A leaderboard screenshot or selected winning trades is insufficient.
2. **Contract and exposure matching:** Exact US instrument/market identifier, asset, family, cadence, boundary times, reference index, side/intent, price, quantity, and settlement result. Group repeated fills in one window into a coherent position episode; do not count every fill as an independent prediction. Handle hedges and simultaneous opposite exposures explicitly. Report 15m and 1h results separately and together.
3. **Win-rate denominator and uncertainty:** Define a win as a completed position episode with positive net trading PnL, with break-even/void/unresolved cases reported separately. Also report outcome accuracy separately if needed. Show wins, losses, total completed episodes, distinct windows/days, coverage fraction and a confidence interval. As a planning illustration, 70 wins in 100 independent episodes gives a roughly 60.4%–78.1% Wilson 95% interval; 700/1000 gives roughly 67.1%–72.8%. These examples are arithmetic, not evidence about any trader. Overlapping BTC windows, multiple trades per event and shared signals reduce effective independence; block results by event/day instead of treating fills as independent samples.
4. **Profitability and risk:** Reconcile cashflows, realized PnL and remaining inventory; separate fees, rebates and incentives. Report gross and net PnL, capital used, ROI definition, average win/loss, maximum loss, drawdown, exposure and concentration. Report trading PnL separately from incentive income. High win rate alone is insufficient: 95 one-contract wins buying at $0.99 and five losses produce `95×$0.01 − 5×$0.99 = −$4.00` before fees.
5. **Source-to-follower delay:** Persist source execution time, source publish time if supplied, first local receive time in UTC, monotonic receive time, decision time, order submission/ack and follower fill time. Measure clock error, ingestion lag and p50/p95/p99 source-to-receipt delay, including replay and reconnect periods. Historical event timestamps do not reveal when a follower could first know the trade. Backtests may act only after recorded first receipt plus measured execution delay.
6. **Copyability:** Reconstruct the executable US bid/ask and depth at receipt, spread, fees, available size, partial fills, rejects and remaining time to settlement. Apply price/age/size limits, missed-exit rules and kill switches. Compare source net PnL with simulated follower net PnL; never assume the source fill price remains available or that resting maker orders can be copied as maker fills.
7. **Selection and survivor bias:** Register selection rules, eligible population and observation period before scoring. Retain inactive, losing and disappeared sources rather than backfilling only today's leaders. Freeze each cohort at its historical selection timestamp; use chronological training/validation/holdout periods and walk-forward paper evaluation. Include all tested candidates/thresholds in the experiment log to expose multiple-testing bias. An opt-in cohort is still selected and cannot be called representative of all US traders.

## Realistic next steps

1. Ask Polymarket US through its documented support/partner channel whether it offers an approved opt-in trader-performance product, authorized source-account data sharing, or a commercial attributable feed. Request a schema, identity scope, historical coverage, correction semantics, measured/contractual delay and redistribution terms. The [API Introduction](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/introduction) supplies the support contact; no message was sent during this research.
2. If cooperating source accounts are available, establish platform-supported entitlements and a complete source ledger before building rankings. Use permissioned history/streaming only within the granted account scope. The [Accounts & Identity guide](https://docs.polymarket.us/trader-guide/accounts-identity) documents the relevant entitlement boundary.
3. Without attributable US source data, keep a US copy-trading ranking empty and explicitly unavailable. Public tape can support anonymous flow/price research, but should be named accordingly and must not invent traders or win rates.
4. Once data exists, construct the dataset above and evaluate delayed copies in paper mode with a cohort frozen in advance. Any later live deployment requires separate evidence that follower returns survive fees, depth, outages and observed receipt delays. This research provides no such performance evidence.

The decisive missing input is **an authorized, stable US trader identity joined to sufficiently complete historical and timely ongoing executions**. More UI or a different leaderboard scraper cannot supply that missing provenance.
